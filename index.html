Visual Identity

The Hudson County visual identity is a complete, interconnected system. Every element, the wordmark, the icon, the color palette, the typography, is designed to work individually and together across every surface, format, and community context.

The system follows a clear architecture: a strong County master brand that departments and municipalities can express within, without fragmenting. The mark anchors the system; the palette gives it range; the typeface gives it legibility; the icon gives it meaning.

Design patterns adapt locally while staying recognizably County. This is the balance the system is engineered to hold.

Logo & mark

County seal

The County Seal is the official emblem of Hudson County and the most authoritative mark in the identity system. It carries the weight of institutional heritage and is used exclusively in official government contexts, formal correspondence, legal documents, official proclamations, and ceremonial applications.

The Seal is not a general-purpose logo. Its use signals formality, authority, and government action. It should never appear in casual or promotional communications, as this dilutes its significance and authority.

Color

A palette built for function, with room for character.

The Hudson County color system moves beyond the traditionally black-and-white posture of government communication. The palette is welcoming, modern, and accessible, designed to carry authority without coldness, and warmth without informality.

The system is structured for range. The primary palette anchors all County-level communication. The secondary palette gives departments space for differentiation without breaking cohesion. Color usage must always maintain sufficient contrast ratios to meet WCAG AA standards.

Color

Primary palette

The primary palette is the anchor of the brand system. Liberty Green, Deep Teal, Black, and White appear across all County communications and establish the visual baseline that every department and touchpoint builds from.

Color

Secondary palette

The secondary palette provides range for departmental differentiation, campaign work, and communications contexts where the primary green would be insufficiently expressive. Secondary colors are always used within the primary system, never independently of it.

Each secondary color maintains sufficient contrast when paired with white or black text, ensuring accessibility standards are met regardless of usage context.

Color

Color combinations & accessibility

Every color combination in the system has been tested against WCAG 2.1 contrast requirements. All approved pairings meet or exceed AAA, the highest standard, for both normal and large text. Never use unapproved combinations or reduce contrast for aesthetic reasons.

Typography

Typography is an essential element of our identity and a powerful tool for achieving consistent expression in our communication.

Contemporary sans-serif is the baseline requirement. Clear hierarchy supports wayfinding and service navigation, residents should be able to scan a page and immediately understand what it is, what they need to do, and where to go next.

Founders Grotesk is a contemporary amalgamation of classic grotesks. Millar & Richard's early 20th century Grotesque series provided rudimentary geometry, serpentine curves, and the narrow but welcome aperture of certain forms.

Further details were inspired by H. W. Caslon's Doric series from the same era and coupled with tight spacing strategies from Helvetica's Halbfett headline metal cuts of the late 1950s. It resolves the best details from the last century into a large family designed for modern typography.

Typography

Typographic usage

Four levels, no exceptions. The system defines four typographic roles, display headlines, section headlines, eyebrow labels, and body copy, each mapped to a specific typeface, weight, size range, tracking, and leading. Mixing roles, weights, or sizes outside this system undermines hierarchy.

Condensed for leadership. Founders Grotesk Condensed Bold carries all large headline and display roles. It is always uppercase, always tracked tight, and never used at body sizes or mixed-case.

No personality fonts. No decorative, script, or display-only typefaces are permitted within the brand system. Functional elegance is the standard.

Google fallback font

When Founders Grotesk is unavailable but web fonts can be loaded, in vendor-built digital tools, third-party platforms, or environments where Founders Grotesk licensing cannot be guaranteed, DM Sans is the designated Google Fonts fallback.

DM Sans is a low-contrast geometric sans-serif optimized for on-screen readability. It shares Founders Grotesk's clean, neutral structure, making it the closest freely available alternative. It is not a replacement for Founders Grotesk in branded communications.

These typographic errors undermine legibility, brand consistency, and the authority of County communications.

  • Avoid extremely tall leading for both heading and body text
  • Avoid overly tight leading, with crashing ascenders and descenders, for both heading and body text
  • Avoid overly tight tracking, with crashing glyphs
  • Avoid overly wide tracking
  • Avoid mixed weights or fonts within a single headline

Photography

Photography that earns its place.

Hudson County's photography direction is documentary in spirit, grounded in the daily life of the county and the communities that make it. Imagery is not used to project aspiration or sell a vision. It is used to reflect reality honestly, and in doing so, build trust.

Photography

Landscape: dos

These are guiding principles when creating landscape photography assets. They're not hard and fast rules, but a starting point for shooting imagery used in print or digital collateral.

Shoot organic compositions where the subject matter does not feel staged.
Play with light and shadow to create visual interest.
Rich and warm color tones make the photography feel human and approachable.
Imperfect and natural colors express a grounded, authentic approach to civic spaces.
Show the architectural character and texture of Hudson County's built environment.
Capture the density and diversity of the county's neighborhoods at street level.
Photography

Landscape: don'ts

Avoid photography choices that undermine authenticity, flatten the county's character, or project an aspirational image that does not reflect the lived experience of Hudson County residents.

Don't shoot desaturated, overly stylized landscapes that strip away the county's warmth and character.
Avoid blown-out, over-exposed images that feel generic and lack a sense of place.
Don't apply heavy color grading or filters that make images feel processed and inauthentic.

Avoid generic stock skylines that could belong to any city, use photography that is specific to Hudson County.

Photography

Community: dos

Community photography should reflect Hudson County's actual residents, at work, at rest, and in service. Documentary instincts over casting. Real moments over posed compositions.

Capture county employees and residents in the actual course of their work and lives.
Shoot organic compositions where subjects don't appear directed or posed.
Prioritize connection and candid interaction over polished portraiture.
Show community members in their own environments, parks, streets, community centers, transit.
Let natural light and environment define the mood rather than studio setups.
Include the full age spectrum of the county, children, working adults, and seniors are all part of the story.

Community: don'ts

Avoid community photography that looks manufactured, performs diversity, or positions the county as aspirational rather than accountable. These approaches erode trust.

Don't desaturate community photography, it distances viewers from the humanity of the subjects.

Iconography

Inspired by our logo and sans-serif typeface Founders, our iconography utilizes simple forms to efficiently communicate ideas.

The Hudson County icon system is built from the same geometric language as the wordmark. Icons are designed on a consistent grid, using strokes and forms that feel native to the brand rather than borrowed from a generic library.
